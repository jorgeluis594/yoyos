# Product identification

Identification decides which product of the company a customer's photo refers to, and how certain that is. It returns only identity and certainty; [message-to-order](message-to-order.md) decides what to do with the chat's open order. Photos use a cascade that stops at the first step that answers: the printed QR label first, then visual search. Texts are not identified here: the reader names the product, and products only supplies it the catalog data it needs ([Products for the reader](#products-for-the-reader)).

Code: `apps/core/src/features/products/application/identify-products-in-image.ts` (cascade), `find-products-for-reading.ts` and `find-message-candidates.ts` (what the reader is given), `catalog-search-readiness.ts`, `index-product-photos.ts` (indexing), `domain/image-identification.ts` (rules), `infrastructure/product-matching/` (embedders, choosers, the dim photo brightening and the post photo crop), `infrastructure/image-embeddings.ts` (vector queries), `infrastructure/text-candidates.ts` and `reading-products.ts` (reader queries). Schema: `ImageEmbedding` in `apps/core/prisma/schema.prisma`.

## Photo cascade

1. **QR.** Read the QR codes of the image.
   - Product QR codes found (a variant UUID, what labels print): their sellable (active) variants are the result, each with its product; a deactivated or deleted variant matches nothing (one item per distinct variant). The lookup ignores visibility: the label of a hidden product is found and reaches the reader marked hidden, which reports it with the hidden reason instead of adding it ([Message to order](message-to-order.md#rules)); the point of sale, which reads the same QR codes for the seller, adds it as usual. Codes that match no sellable variant are reported as product not found.
   - Only non-product QR codes (Yape/Plin payments, URLs): the photo is not a product. Reason: such a QR says the image is not a product photo.
   - Unreadable image: stop.
   - No QR code at all: continue to visual search.
2. **Visual.** Only when visual search is configured and the company has at least one indexed product, that is, any current photo of an active, visible product with an active variant has an embedding of the model (checked before any embedding, so a company without an indexed catalog costs nothing).
   1. If the image is dim (its luminance deviation is under 28: dark, blurry or washed out), stretch its contrast. If it is a screenshot of a social post or chat (a photo band with flat app screen above and below it), cut that photo out. Embed the photo cut out, or the brightened copy, or the whole image otherwise.
   2. Take the K nearest active, offered (sellable and not hidden) products of the company by cosine distance (`PRODUCT_MATCH_CANDIDATES`, default 5). Every current photo of a product is compared; a product counts once, at the distance of its closest photo, even if several of its photos match.
   3. A vision model receives the customer image as it came, the photo cut out of it or its brightened copy if any, and, per candidate, that closest photo, labelled `A`, `B`, …, and answers one label or `none` with a confidence, plus the probability that the image's main subject is an item a shop could sell (`shows_product`): alone, worn, held, on a screen, in a social post screenshot, or dark, blurry or cropped; not a selfie or portrait, a building or place, a payment or transfer, a chat, a map, a receipt, a document or a meme.
   4. `shows_product` below 0.5 means the photo is not a product, whatever the choice and its confidence. Reason: a selfie in a knitted sweater can resemble a catalog sweater, and a photo that is not a product is no purchase intent.
   5. Otherwise accept the choice only if it is one of the candidates and its confidence is at least `PRODUCT_MATCH_MIN_CONFIDENCE` (default 0.8). The result is the product, not a variant. With no accepted choice, it is a product that was not found.
   Products without active variants and hidden products are not candidates (hidden products keep being indexed, so a product shown again is recognized at once), and a company with none answers `no_indexed_products`. A photo of a hidden product without its QR label can therefore match a visible look-alike ([Message to order · Known limitations](message-to-order.md#known-limitations)).

Results: `found` (QR items or the visual product, with method and confidence), `not_product`, or `not_found` with its reason (`unreadable_image`, `unrecognized_qr_code`, `product_not_found`, `visual_search_disabled`, `no_indexed_products`, `no_visual_match`, `low_confidence`). A visual match returns at most one product per image (a live screenshot shows the product being offered) and is added like a QR match, without a confirmation step.

## Products for the reader

Texts have no identification step. The reader ([message-to-order](message-to-order.md#what-the-reader-receives-and-returns)) names the product from the products it is given, and Yoyos only checks the product is one of them and still sellable. Products owns two reading capabilities:

- `findProductsForReading` (`application/find-products-for-reading.ts`, `infrastructure/reading-products.ts`, type `ProductForReading` in `domain/reading-product.ts`): for a list of product ids, in one read snapshot with a fixed number of queries, each product's name, whether it is hidden, whether it is sellable (active, not hidden, with at least one active variant), the values of each attribute in stored option order and its active variants. A hidden product is returned marked hidden and not sellable, with no variants and no attributes, so no reader rule offers its variants and the reader can tell hidden from no longer sold. A deleted product is absent.
- `findMessageCandidates` (`application/find-message-candidates.ts`, `infrastructure/text-candidates.ts`): up to `TEXT_MATCH_CANDIDATES` (default 8) active, visible products with a sellable variant whose names cover most of a text's words. The words are the text's distinct words of 3 or more characters (lower case, accents stripped, split on non-letters and non-digits), longest first, capped from the company's visible active product count by `TEXT_MATCH_PAIR_BUDGET` (25,000 word × product pairs) between `TEXT_MATCH_MIN_WORDS` (3) and `TEXT_MATCH_MAX_WORDS` (30). One statement under the company's RLS adds `word_similarity` over the matching words at or above `TEXT_MATCH_MIN_SIMILARITY` (default 0.3) and ranks by that sum, then the shorter name, then the id. A statement over 1 s returns no candidates and no error. No model is called.

## Variant resolution

Code resolves the variant from the values the reader chose, for photos and texts:

- A QR-selected variant with no other values → that variant.
- One sellable variant → that variant.
- Values are compared with the catalog's attribute names and values ignoring case and accents. Sellable variants are filtered by the known values: one left → that variant; none → not available; several → the attributes that still vary are missing («qué talla o color», in the product's stored attribute order).
- Unnamed attributes keep the base values: the QR variant, the item being changed, or a pending's known values.
- No code matches buyer words against values or word lists (founder rule): the product serves Spanish and Portuguese buyers and any catalog, so understanding the words is the reader's job, and code only checks its answer against the catalog.

A visual product with no sellable variant is reported as not identified. Reason for one authority: product images and names identify a product, so the variant always comes from the buyer's words.

## Catalog search readiness

`GET /api/products/search-readiness` (session, `no-store`, contract `shared/contracts/catalog-search-readiness.ts`) answers `no_active_products` (no product with an active variant), `all_products_hidden` (products with an active variant exist, none visible), `no_searchable_photos` (visual search disabled, or no current photo of an offered product has an embedding of the configured model) or `ready`. Presence is read with two short-circuit `EXISTS` in one statement, never an aggregate over every product, because it runs on every miss and every focus of the mobile screen. Message-to-order consults it only on miss paths, to keep misses silent while visual search cannot answer (text misses are silent for `no_active_products` and `all_products_hidden`); the mobile WhatsApp screen shows the matching hint.

## Indexing

- Every photo of a product is indexed, one embedding per photo, when the product is saved with it. A photo uploaded but never saved to a product, or discarded with the form, identifies nothing.
- A create or save whose photo list holds an image the product did not have publishes `product_photos_added { companyId, productId }` after commit. Removing or reordering photos publishes nothing: a removed photo stops identifying the product as soon as the save without it commits, because search joins the product's current photos and never scans embeddings by themselves (embeddings of removed photos stay).
- The `index-product-photos` handler tries every photo of the product without an embedding of the model, one at a time in position order, and never stops at a failed photo. The job is retried (up to 8 times) only when some photo failed with a retryable code; a retry skips photos indexed meanwhile. Permanent failures (`INVALID_EMBEDDING`, `IMAGE_NOT_FOUND`) are logged per photo and the job succeeds. Reason: one bad photo must never keep the product's other photos unindexed (rejected: stopping the job at the first failure). Meanwhile the product is recognizable by its indexed photos.
- The event carries ids, not images, so a late event never indexes a removed photo for search; a photo removed while its job runs gets an embedding that no product uses, which is harmless. Indexing is idempotent per image and model.
- A product becomes recognizable by new photos after a delay (the job runs after the save, photo by photo), not at once.
- A failed publication does not fail the product save; it is logged and recovered with `pnpm --dir apps/core products:index-images`, which publishes the event for every active product with any photo lacking an embedding of the configured model. A photo that always fails permanently is tried once more on every run, without blocking the others.
- Indexing runs one job at a time. Reason: the local model saturates the worker CPU; one indexing job keeps a catalog upload from slowing customer image searches and bounds memory. The queue is shared by every company, so a product with 8 photos holds it for up to 8 embeddings; run `products:index-images` off-peak.
- Logs: `product_photos_indexed` (info, with `indexedCount`, `failedCount`, `durationMs` and `queueLatencyMs`, the wait from the event to the handler, so «recognizable within minutes» is read end to end), `product_photos_index_skipped` (debug), `product_photos_added_publish_failed` (error) and `product_photo_index_failed` once per failed photo and attempt. A retryable failure is a warning until the last attempt, then an error. A permanent failure is an error on the job's first attempt and debug on later retries of the same job, so one bad photo yields one error line per job. Known limit: a permanent failure first seen on a retry (the photo failed retryably before) is logged at debug only.

## Decisions

| Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Identification is owned by `products` and returns identity and certainty only; `orders` decides and applies the order change | The QR code, image or text identifies a product, not an order change. Dependency direction stays orders → products. |
| Embeddings belong to the image (`ImageEmbedding` keyed by company, image and model), not the product | Products sharing an image share its embedding, an image change only needs the new image embedded, and embeddings of customer images can be cached later. |
| pgvector in the same PostgreSQL, exact search, no ANN index | Catalogs have hundreds of products per company; an exact scan is cheaper than maintaining an index. Revisit beyond about 10,000 embeddings per company, which with 8 photos per product is about 1,250 products; an ANN index is not a drop-in, because it orders photos, not products, so it needs an over-fetch (about K × 8 photos, then one per product) and a filter to current photos; the hidden-product filter is a second one applied after the scan, so the over-fetch must also grow with the company's hidden share, or a mostly-hidden store would get too few candidates. |
| No index for the hidden filter | Every read that filters `hidden = false` already scans the company's products and tests a per-row predicate; `hidden` is one more column test on rows already read and only removes rows. Measured on 10,000 products under forced RLS, with every product visible and with 90% hidden, the plans are identical with and without it. A partial index led by `companyId` is the alternative to test if the 90%-hidden shape ever regresses. |
| The candidate query scans the company's products through `Product (companyId)` and caps the searched words by catalog size | The trigram GIN cannot serve the app role: `Product` has forced RLS and the pg_trgm operators are not leakproof. Marking them leakproof and a `btree_gin` index were rejected (a security judgment on extension functions; RLS still prevents the index from serving the matches). Past about 10,000 products the cost passes 150 ms; the way beyond is a follow-up. |
| The chooser sees one photo per candidate, the one closest to the customer image | A customer photo of the product worn is compared with the worn photo, not the front one, at the cost of one photo per candidate, as with a single photo. |
| Every embedding stores its model; search compares only embeddings of the configured model | Vectors of different models are not comparable. Changing the embedder or its model requires deploying and running `products:index-images`. |
| Default embedder: local SigLIP 2 base (`onnx-community/siglip2-base-patch16-224-ONNX`, fp32) in the worker | One local inference instead of paid calls, deterministic, and no image leaves the server. Among commercially licensed models it gave the best preselection recall within the worker's latency and memory limits; quantized exports lost recall on screenshots, and OpenAI CLIP was excluded for its license. Photo throughput is bound by the worker's vCPU, not by job concurrency. |
| Alternative embedder `IMAGE_EMBEDDER_PROVIDER=openai`: a vision model describes the image, a text embedding model embeds the description | OpenAI offers no image embedding endpoint. |
| A post or chat screenshot is searched by the photo inside it, found by fixed rules on pixel rows; the vision model still sees the whole screenshot | The app screen around a reposted catalog photo lowered the chooser's certainty under 0.8 for the very photo the seller uploaded. Rules cost milliseconds and no call; the whole screenshot keeps payment and chat screens recognisable as non-products. |
| A dim photo is searched by a contrast-stretched copy, and the vision model sees that copy beside the photo as it came | Dark, blurred photos of real products were answered `none` or under 0.8. Showing only the brightened copy rescued one and lost another, whose colours it shifted; with both views every damaged fixture photo is found. Clear photos are never touched (all fixtures are above a deviation of 30); a flat map screenshot is, and stays a non-product. Matching still needs 0.8, so a dim photo that is not a product gains nothing. |
| No proximity threshold on candidates | Distances are measured first: visual outcomes log `topDistance` and `chosenDistance` to choose a cutoff later, and `chosenPhotoPosition` shows whether a photo other than the primary identified the product. |
| Captions are read by the reader, never used for identification; product names are not sent to the vision model | A caption never chooses another product than the photo's, and it stays out of the identification request; names bias towards text and are seller data the vision model does not need. The reader receives the caption with the identified product's name and variants as text, never the image. |
| A photo that does not show a product is no purchase intent | Payment and transfer screenshots ask for nothing; notifying them would bury real misses. |
| Method and certainty of each identification are kept in the message's `ChatMessageOutcome`, not only in logs | They make each message apply once and are the data for showing how an item was identified and for threshold tuning. |
| `INVALID_MATCHER_RESPONSE` and `INVALID_EMBEDDING` are permanent failures; provider unavailability is retried | Their causes repeat on every paid attempt, so retrying only multiplies cost. |
| Provider adapters sit behind the `EmbedImage` and `ChooseProduct` ports, chosen by environment, and pass shared contract tests | Providers can be swapped or compared without touching the cascade. |

## Configuration

Visual search requires `OPENAI_API_KEY` (the chooser calls the OpenAI API). Without it both indexing and the visual step are disabled and images without QR end as `visual_search_disabled`; development and tests run without it. Other variables and their defaults are declared in `apps/core/src/features/products/composition.ts`: `PRODUCT_MATCHER_PROVIDER`, `PRODUCT_MATCHER_MODEL`, `IMAGE_EMBEDDER_PROVIDER`, `IMAGE_EMBEDDER_MODEL`, `IMAGE_DESCRIBE_MODEL`, `PRODUCT_MATCH_CANDIDATES`, `PRODUCT_MATCH_MIN_CONFIDENCE`, `PRODUCT_MATCH_TIMEOUT_MS`, `TEXT_MATCH_CANDIDATES`, `TEXT_MATCH_MIN_SIMILARITY`, `TEXT_MATCH_PAIR_BUDGET`, `TEXT_MATCH_MIN_WORDS`, `TEXT_MATCH_MAX_WORDS`. An unknown provider name fails at startup. Each chooser call is bounded by `PRODUCT_MATCH_TIMEOUT_MS`; the image cascade does not receive the message job's attempt deadline, so its bound is that timeout and the job's expiry.

The local model files are not committed; `pnpm --dir apps/core models:fetch` downloads them (see [apps/core/models/README.md](../apps/core/models/README.md)).

Log events: `product_visual_match_completed` (method, confidence, distances, `showsProduct`, `detail`: `post_photo`, `brightened` or null), `product_visual_search_disabled`, `message_candidates_found` and `message_candidates_timeout` (word and match counts, duration). The reader receives product names and variant values, because the buyer's words are compared with them ([message-to-order](message-to-order.md)). Never logged: image bytes, prompts, raw model responses, captions, buyer words, product names, vectors or keys. Customer images and product photos sent to providers must not be retained for training; zero-retention options are used where the provider offers them.
