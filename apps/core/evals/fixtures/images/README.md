# Synthetic photo fixtures for orders from chat

This set tests product detection, visual catalog matching, and printed variant QR labels for the fictional Yoyos seller in `../catalog.ts`. Every image is synthetic: generated with GPT Image 2, or composited from those generated images with Sharp and ZXing WASM. The people are generated fictional people. No real customer photos, personal data, phone numbers, bank accounts, or national ID documents were used. Names, handles, amounts, operation codes, and locations are invented.

## Contents

- `catalog/`: 10 seller catalog photos, one for each product that has a photo in the reference catalog. Four accepted catalog photos existed before this continuation and were kept without regeneration.
- `customer/`: 55 buyer images. For each photographed catalog product there is a worn view, a market-stall/in-hand view, a repost screenshot, a photo of another screen, and a degraded low-light crop. Five products also have a worn view in their other listed color.
- `negative/`: 10 near-miss or unsold products; expected visual result is no match at the 0.8 certainty threshold.
- `non-product/`: 10 payment, chat, map, receipt, selfie, delivery-reference, meme, and payment-QR images; the image pass expects `not_product` from the detection cascade, and in a message they lead to no purchase intent.
- `label/`: 2 printed variant tags. The QR codes were generated by ZXing WASM from `variantIdOf()` values in `../catalog.ts`, composited onto generated tag photos, and read back from the final JPEGs with ZXing WASM.
- `manifest.json`: one row per fixture with source, scenario, and expected behavior.

The new buyer views were edited from or composed with their catalog references so color, cut, stripes, mesh, knit, and buckles remain recognizable. Repost and screen views deliberately use JPEG damage, glare, moiré, or angle; the `bad` views use blur, low exposure, and cropping. UI screenshots were typeset programmatically for readable invented text. The receipt's first generated text was too faint and malformed, so it was replaced with readable synthetic text on the generated paper photo. Draft screen composites with a product image outside the device border and draft label composites with QR codes outside the tag were corrected before delivery.

This continuation used **48 image generations**: 6 remaining catalog photos, 10 worn edits, 5 color edits, 10 market-stall edits, 10 negative products, 4 non-product photo bases, 2 blank tag photos, and 1 cat photo. The 4 earlier accepted catalog generations are additional. Compositing and corrections used no additional image generation. The screen and app UI composites are synthetic approximations and may look cleaner than real chat uploads; plain black basic and premium shirts remain intentionally difficult to distinguish visually.

## Rights and use

Use these fixtures for internal evaluation under the applicable OpenAI image-generation terms and the project's policies. Payment app names identify fictional mock screens; no real app screenshot or logo was copied. This set is not evidence of a real transaction, person, seller, or location. Do not treat synthetic photo matches as proof of model accuracy on real customer uploads.

## How the evals read it

`manifest.json` is the only thing the evals read (`../image-manifest.ts` validates it with Zod; `pnpm --dir apps/core test:unit`
parses it and checks that every listed file exists):

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `file`, `folder` | The file name inside its folder (or a path relative to this folder). |
| `productKey`, `color` | The catalog product and color shown, or null; required for `match` and `label`. |
| `scenario` | What the photo shows (`worn`, `in_hand`, `repost`, `screen`, `bad`, `color_variant_worn`, `printed_qr_label`, …); reported as kind. |
| `expected` | `match` (the product is found), `no_match` (not identified), `not_product` (nothing changes), `label` (found by its QR, in that color). |
| `generator`, `prompt`, `derivedFrom` | The source of the photo. |

`catalog/` photos are indexed with the real embedder as the seller's photos and are never used as queries. Every other entry is one
image case; the end-to-end cases pick their photos by product, folder and a hint. Buyer photos of the Polo básico and the Polo
premium (near-identical plain tees) pass as the exact product or as not identified and are reported as the «indistinguishable pair».

## Real captures

Real payment screenshots or buyer photos go only in `../images-local/` (gitignored), with their own `manifest.json` in the same
format (`EVAL_LOCAL_IMAGES_DIR` names another folder). They are merged at run time, never index the catalog, and their cases are
tagged `local`. **Never commit real captures**, or copy them into this folder.

The eval cases also reuse two CC0 fixtures that already exist in the repository (sources in their own READMEs):

- `src/features/products/infrastructure/product-matching/embedding-fixtures/product-a-front.jpg` (The Met Open Access, CC0): a product the catalog does not sell.
- `src/features/products/infrastructure/qr-fixtures/non-uuid-qr.jpg` (generated): a payment QR whose content is a URL.
